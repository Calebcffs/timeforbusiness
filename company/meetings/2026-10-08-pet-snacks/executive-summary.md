### The licence, in plain English

**The regulator is AVS (NParks), not SFA.** Our earlier setup notes said SFA. That was wrong for pet treats, and `04-setup-costs.md` is now corrected.

| What you would sell | What you need |
|---|---|
| Non-meat treats (fruit, vegetable) | A "no meat" declaration from the maker and a S$22 permit per shipment. This is the lightest route. |
| Meat treats from Australia, NZ, Canada, UK or US | No pre-approval. A vet health certificate and the permit. |
| Heat-treated meat from the EU or Japan | An online self-declaration, a certificate and the permit. |
| Freeze-dried or raw meat from anywhere else (for example China or Thailand) | The factory's dossier approved by AVS, which takes an estimated 4 to 12 weeks. We doubt it is legal for some origins. |
| Making it yourself in Singapore | An AVS licence (about S$240 a year) and commercial premises. Home kitchens are not allowed. |

**Still unknown:** whether fish and insect count as "meat", and whether an importer needs an extra licence. Only AVS can answer. We drafted five questions for you to send.
